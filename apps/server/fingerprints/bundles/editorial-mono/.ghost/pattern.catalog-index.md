## Composition

When a surface must hold many parallel entries — options surveyed, sources
consulted, items ranked, findings archived — Editorial Mono sets them as a
printed catalog index, not a card grid or a paginated feed. The grammar comes
from the index pages of well-set reference books: every entry numbered, every
row on the same measure, the whole collection legible at once, and order doing
the work that decoration does elsewhere.

**Numbered specimen rows.** Each entry is a ruled row opened by a mono counter —
`01` `02` `03` — set in `--editorial-entry-number-font` at
`--editorial-entry-number-size` with `--editorial-entry-number-tracking`, flush
to the left margin like a folio in reverse. The counter is [mono metadata](pattern.metadata)
furniture: it registers position in the sequence, and when the sequence is a
ranking, the number *is* the verdict of the row. Rows share one measure — name,
counter, criteria, and implication land in the same columns on every row, split
by `--editorial-column-rule` where they run parallel — so the reader compares by
scanning down a column, never by hunting across differently shaped cards.

**Specimen treatment.** Where an entry earns display weight — the recommended
item, the headline figure, the exemplar — set its key fact as a typographic
specimen: the serif at `--editorial-specimen-size` and
`--editorial-specimen-leading`, with a mono caption beneath in the folio
discipline naming what the figure is. The specimen is a type-specimen sheet's
move: the material itself, shown large, labeled small. One specimen per catalog
region at most; a page of specimens is a poster, not an index.

**Everything visible, sorted by position.** The catalog's tactile promise is
that the whole collection is on the sheet at once. Filtering and ranking read as
*rearrangement*, not removal: entries that fall outside the current criterion
demote down the sequence or set back to `--editorial-disabled-ink` on
`--editorial-disabled-paper`, but they stay on the page, because what was
excluded is part of the record. Never collapse the index behind pagination,
accordions, or a "show more" control when the entries would fit a scroll —
a printed index does not hide its own pages.

**Revealed measure.** A catalog may show its own construction: faint
`--editorial-guide-rule` lines extending from the shared column edges toward the
page margins, like the drawn guides on a paste-up board. The guides are quieter
than `--editorial-hairline-faint` and carry no interaction — they exist so the
alignment reads as deliberate, a compositor's discipline made visible. Use them
at most once per surface, framing the catalog region, never as an all-over
overlay that competes with the ruled paper grid.

The catalog rows are [ruled evidence bands](pattern.evidence) run at collection scale;
the counters, captions, and guide labels speak in the [mono metadata](pattern.metadata)
voice; inside a long document the catalog is the **evidence table** or **source
list** block of the [dossier structure](pattern.dossier-structure); and the one entry
the page recommends may be promoted out of the index into the
[single inverse-ink panel](pattern.inverse-panel) while keeping its counter, so the
verdict still cites its place in the sequence.
