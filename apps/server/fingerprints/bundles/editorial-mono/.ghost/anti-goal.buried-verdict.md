description: Not neutral inventories that open with Overview titles, evidence grids, or competing claim panels; instead a masthead or inverse slab that states the verdict first — recognize the switch by generic headings and detail appearing before the recommendation.
---

**Not X:** the neutral-inventory default — a surface that opens with a generic
title (Overview, Summary, Dashboard, Report) and no claim attached; evidence
tables, item grids, or paragraphs appearing before the recommendation or
outcome; several equal-weight claim panels competing instead of one editorial
hierarchy; a close written as a passive recap that restates the masthead
without adding judgment.

**Instead Y:** the page commits before it explains. Open with the masthead /
deck / folio shell — the verdict headline in serif display at the largest scale
on the page, tight `--leading-display`, negative `--tracking-display` — or with
the single inverse ink panel, so the claim is the first visual event. The deck
beneath **interprets** the claim rather than restating it. Caveats and risks
demote into ruled evidence bands with tracked mono labels (Risk, Caveat,
Accepted tradeoff). The page lands on an editorial close — a recommendation,
accepted tradeoff, or single next action in one calm committed line, shorter
and stronger than the evidence above it.

**Recognize the switch by:**

- the first heading is a category name, not a claim — it could sit atop any
  page in the product without being wrong;
- the largest type on the page labels a section rather than stating the verdict;
- a table, KPI row, or card grid appears in the first major region while the
  recommendation waits below it;
- more than one panel wears verdict-weight emphasis (inverse ink, framing
  rules, or display scale), so no single claim reads first;
- the closing region summarizes what the reader already saw instead of
  committing to a decision, risk, or next action.

The fix is editorial, not cosmetic: find the one claim the page exists to make,
promote it into the masthead or the inverse slab, and re-order everything else
as evidence beneath it.
