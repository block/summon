description: The verdict earns the page — state the recommendation, rank, risk, or outcome before any supporting detail, spend the page's one emphasis on it, and demote everything else into ruled evidence; pull this first when deciding what a surface opens with.
---

Editorial Mono is strongest when the page is organized around a verdict, not a
neutral inventory of facts. The page has already made up its mind: the
recommendation, ranking, risk, or outcome is stated **before** supporting
detail, and everything after it exists to justify that claim, not to compete
with it.

**The stance, exactly:**

- The verdict appears in the **first major visual region** — the masthead
  headline in serif display at the largest scale on the page, or the single
  inverse slab (`--editorial-verdict-bg` on `--editorial-verdict-fg`). Never
  below the fold, never after an evidence table.
- The verdict gets the page's **one emphasis budget**: the largest type, or the
  one inverse panel, or the one paired-hairline frame — never all three, and
  never spent twice. If two moments compete, the verdict wins and the other
  demotes to a ruled evidence band.
- Neutral titles — Overview, Summary, Dashboard, Report — never stand alone.
  Either pair them with a real claim or replace them with the claim itself.
- Caveats, risks, and tradeoffs **demote into evidence bands** beneath the
  verdict, labeled in tracked mono (Risk, Caveat, Accepted tradeoff), so they
  support the decision rather than postponing it.
- The page **closes on the verdict resolved into action** — a recommendation,
  accepted tradeoff, or next step, shorter and stronger than the evidence — not
  a passive recap.

**When the task shape varies, the principle does not.** A recommendation reads
first as a claim in serif display; a metrics surface leads with one governing
number, rank, or delta in the inverse slab, never four equal KPI cards; a
comparison marks the chosen option by position, border weight, or an ink label
before the criteria columns begin. Completeness must never bury the verdict
under equal-weight criteria, paragraphs, or widgets.

**When the surface is operational rather than judgmental** — a form, a settings
screen, a login — there is no verdict to promote: the masthead demotes to a
section heading in the serif voice, the inverse slab goes unspent (it is a
budget, not a quota), and the page's discipline is carried by the ruled controls
and mono labels alone.

The reader should understand the verdict, the criteria, and the next action
before reading a single paragraph of detail. If they have to infer the claim
from the evidence, the page has failed regardless of how well it is set.
