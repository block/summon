## Not this

Decisive Dark refuses evidence that decorates instead of proving:

- **Not** smoothed line charts, decorative waves, vague blobs, or gradient
  data art. **Instead** discrete `--dd-bar` bars and candlesticks at
  `--dd-bar-gap` rhythm, heights the eye can compare instantly — a market
  chart reads like real candlestick data, never a friendly curve.
- **Not** dense multicolor palettes where every series gets equal emphasis.
  **Instead** `--dd-bar-neutral` (`--color-text-decorative`) for every series,
  with `--dd-bar` accent reserved for one positive trend and only if it is the
  surface's single [accent moment](pattern.accent-moment).
- **Not** orphaned stats without axis labels, units, or a so-what. **Instead**
  `--dd-axis` labels on the endpoints and anchors, the unit tight to the
  number in `--dd-tnum` tabular figures, and the required [one-sentence
  meaning](pattern.meaning) naming what the data means.
- **Not** evidence buried after long prose, framed in panel fills, or drawn in
  with construction animation. **Instead** the chart sits directly beneath the
  [answer](pattern.answer), frameless on the open canvas, arriving whole —
  final bars, final heights, final axis.

## Recognize the switch

You have slid into soft smeared evidence the moment a chart is pretty before
it is legible: a curve with no visible data points, a gradient fill standing
in for magnitude, a legend the reader must study, or a figure with no unit and
no claim attached. The test is the one-glance read — cover the labels; if the
shape of the data no longer states the trend, the visualization has stopped
being the answer. The fix is discretization and deletion, never annotation:
sharper bars first, then only the labels the eye cannot infer.

The replacement discipline lives in the [evidence block](pattern.evidence),
policed by [deletion discipline](principle.deletion-discipline).
