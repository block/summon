## Guard

Technical Noir's darkness is a working material — a brown-warmed near-charcoal
that reads as a dimmed terminal at 2am. Three costumes keep trying to replace
it, and all three are refused.

**Not pure-black terminal nostalgia.** No `#000` / `#000000` page canvas or
panel fill, no neon-green-on-black hacker cosplay, no phosphor glow, no
retro-monitor scanline effects, no character-rain backdrops. **Instead:** the
warm `--color-bg` (`#2b2622`) canvas with panels lifted only ~8% to
`--color-surface` (`#383330`), off-white `--color-text` (`#f7f5f0`) as the one
ink, and the felt `--noir-grid` as the only permitted texture — felt, never
seen as a pattern. **Recognize the switch by:** any fill at or near true black,
any saturated green/cyan terminal text, any glow or bloom on type or borders.

**Not cinematic noir mood.** No spotlights, fog, film grain, smoked-glass
panels, dramatic shadows, or detective-board metaphors — "noir" here means
restraint, contrast, and focus, never atmosphere detached from task evidence.
**Instead:** presence through hierarchy, alignment, silence, and proof; depth
through the 1px `--noir-hairline` alone; every atmospheric choice tied to task
clarity. **Recognize the switch by:** non-inert shadow values, backdrop blur,
grain overlays, or any darkness that exists for mood rather than legibility.

**Not a light-mode skin.** No white or beige page background with dark text, no
re-skin of a researched source site's light-page expression, no cool-slate dark
mode that drops the warm temperature, no page that opts out of
`color-scheme: dark`. **Instead:** the off-white-on-warm-dark relationship on
every surface — dark warm canvas, off-white primary text and primary action
fill, warmth standing in for a chromatic brand accent. **Recognize the switch
by:** a bright canvas anywhere, dark body text, or a gray palette with no brown
temperature in it.

The test is simple: strip the labels and squint. If the surface reads as a warm
workbench under low light, it is Technical Noir. If it reads as a movie set, a
hacker screensaver, or daytime SaaS, the costume has taken over.
